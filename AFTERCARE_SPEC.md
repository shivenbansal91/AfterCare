# AfterCare

A personal medicine and care organizer. Upload a PDF or photo, or enter medicines manually; review details together and explicitly confirm personal reminder settings. Preserve source evidence and never invent missing instructions. The current revision below supersedes historical milestones.

## Current additions — 2026-10-20

Home reminder cards page one at a time with left/right swipes and tappable position dots underneath. Keep the existing nested vertical scrolling and reminder actions intact; do not present reminders as a free-scrolling slider.

Keep uploaded and manually entered medicines visible together across saved records for the selected person; preserve distinct report/group evidence and existing overlapping-plan protection. Home shows multiple pending reminders in the supplied horizontal carousel. Users can add their own appointments without a prescription. These are explicitly manual records with no original file and reuse review, tracking, sharing, export, archive and encrypted backup behavior. Visits combine manual appointments and source-listed follow-ups for the selected person without inferring bookings from source dates. Caretakers have a discoverable People you support list, shared journey Home, recorded-status counts and patient-filtered updates. Existing grants and revocation remain authoritative. Phone packaging/offline storage/local notification delivery remain separate work; do not introduce an external AI service for patient documents.

## Current agreed revision — 2026-10-19

Additional practical-use changes: medicines can be entered without uploading a file. Manual records retain user attribution, have no original document or fabricated evidence, and reuse review/reminder/permission/export/backup/deletion behavior. The Home profile initial opens account settings, replacing the duplicate Home settings icon. Named meal/sleep instructions use the saved routine; explicit minute/hour gaps are calculated and preserved, while unspecified before/after gaps use editable routine anchors without inventing a delay. Medicine explanations display report problem/diagnosis context, but claim a specific purpose only when that medicine's own confirmed detail states it or the user enters it. Local Phi may select reference facts with that local context; unsupported generated links never become assertions.

The user explicitly replaced the clinician-gated product with a personal prescription organizer. Normal flow: automatic local extraction, one editable overview, explicit user confirmation, suitable user-chosen reminder times and care tracking. No clinician desk or repeated field confirmations in normal UI. Preserve original evidence and mark manual additions. Wake/meal/sleep routine can suggest flexible reminder times but cannot invent dose, frequency, required intervals or a patient's indication. PRN medicines are manually tracked, not converted to fixed repeating doses. Source edits suspend existing reminders until reconfirmed.

Home centers on the next dose and today's medicines with Add prescription. Default to a private personal profile/episode created internally; hide recovery organization from the main flow while retaining existing records and access controls. Caretakers can receive owner-selected taken/skipped/not-confirmed updates after acceptance; absent confirmation is never proof of a missed dose. Owners can revoke access and change alert delay. Report deletion is directly discoverable and explains stopping associated reminders. Medicine explanations use a local source-attributed reference catalog with conservative matching; local Phi may select supported facts but cannot invent medical information or infer personal diagnoses. Custom training and the full synthetic benchmark are deferred. Existing meaningful correctness/permission tests remain required.

The V2 clinician material below is historical and is superseded by this agreed revision. Encrypted storage, backup/restore and local processing remain requirements. No public deployment or guaranteed browser notification delivery is claimed.

## Delivery

Current target: AfterCare V2, requested explicitly after Local 1.0. V2 includes the existing source-linked review, manual medication logs, follow-up bookings, contacts and caregiver permissions, plus a separate clinician review desk, exact time-zone-aware medication schedules, dose records/history/undo, snoozing, foreground notifications and optional background Web Push. Patient review never activates a schedule. Clinical authorization must be provisioned offline by an administrator who independently verifies identity and registration, records evidence and an expiry; no user-facing role switch or self-approval is allowed. Demonstration credentials are restricted to server-generated fictional documents and all resulting schedules are labeled as simulated.

Approval binds the immutable proposal and the report's source/review revision. Source edits, archiving, owner withdrawal, clinician withdrawal, credential revocation/expiry and backup restore block schedules and future alerts. Stop/revoke is permanent for that proposal; restarting requires fresh consent and review. Draft dose, times, start/end dates and IANA time zone are entered explicitly. No clock times, dates or PRN frequency are inferred. DST gaps are omitted and ambiguous clocks occur once; this behavior must be considered by the reviewing clinician.

V2 also includes authenticated encryption of the complete live SQLite snapshot and original documents, Windows-user-bound local key protection or an administrator-managed deployment key, encrypted backup/restore under a new key, owner-only permanent report deletion with password/name confirmation, and an HTTPS deployment configuration. Single-process encrypted snapshot storage is intended for a small installation, not a multi-worker hospital service. Clinical identity verification, clinical validation and actual public hosting are operational tasks, not outcomes a software demo can claim. Browser/OS notification permission, running server, connectivity and push-provider behavior affect delivery; alerts are not an emergency channel.

The following milestones describe implementation history; earlier statements that V2 features were unavailable describe those earlier releases, not the current target.

First slice: fictional sample PDF, PDF text/coordinates, optional local Tesseract OCR, rule baseline, optional local Ollama extraction, field review and audit history, SQLite persistence, printable reviewed plan. V2 adds medication logs, clinically verified schedules, follow-ups, contacts and caregiver authorization. V2 is not complete with the first slice.

Second milestone: local OCR installed and tested on clean scan/photo fixtures; manual medication event logging with immutable snapshots and undo; separate user-entered follow-up bookings and test completion. No reminder scheduler or clinical-verification claim is introduced. Caregiver permissions, contacts and the wider desktop layout remain outstanding.

Third milestone: local username/password accounts with expiring, revocable cookie sessions; account-owned patient profiles and recovery episodes; report ownership enforced for source files, previews, review, plans and activity; saved patient contacts; Today screen for the active report. Existing pre-account reports stay inaccessible until the first local account explicitly imports them into an episode. This migration assumes a trusted owner of the local installation. Storage encryption, account recovery, caregiver delegation and production deployment remain outstanding. Profiles and episodes organize records; they do not clinically verify identity or instructions.

Fourth milestone: patient-scoped caregiver invitations for existing local usernames, seven-day invitation expiry, recipient acceptance/decline, owner revocation, and view-only or view-and-record permissions. Accepted access includes current and future episodes, source documents, reviewed plans, contacts and activity. Caregivers cannot upload, review/edit reports, manage contacts or delegate access. Recorders can log medication events, undo their own events and update visits; owners can undo any medication record. New events identify the actor and access/activity changes have an owner-visible audit. Legacy event authors remain unknown. Revocation blocks new requests and record writes recheck permissions in their transaction; already downloaded/printed copies cannot be recalled. This is local account sharing, not remote synchronization or an email invitation service. No clinical-verification workflow or medication schedule is introduced.

Prescription-reading fix: common prescription labels and supported ruled tables now work with the local reader. OCR reads table cells independently and retains their source boxes, including wrapped instructions, explicit concentrations and duration qualifiers. Local Phi supplements the deterministic reader; it does not regroup parsed table rows. The supplied prescription JPG yields 12 pending fields in both modes. Date of birth is not turned into age, and prescription date is not turned into discharge date. Zero-field uploads distinguish unreadable text from an unrecognized layout and can be explicitly retried by their owner without overwriting later extraction/review. App-owned dropdowns use styled, accessible, phone-scoped select menus. These are reliability/usability fixes, not clinical validation or a general document-accuracy claim.

Fifth milestone: owner-only portable report exports and reversible archive/restore. ZIPs retain the original document, source evidence, review history, all medication events including undone records, current visits and related audit. They exclude credentials, sessions, grants and contacts, are unencrypted, and cannot be imported as account backups. Archived reports disappear from active lists and ordinary APIs for owners and caregivers; only owners can list archive metadata or restore. Restoration preserves report state and respects current grants. SQLite transactions prevent in-flight writes from changing an archived report, reject stale plan saves and conflicting field edits, and preserve concurrent corrections to other fields. No permanent deletion, encryption, clinical verification or medication schedule is introduced.

Sixth milestone: an action-oriented Today flow for the selected report, a source-linked care-question list, clearly separated account and patient/recovery panels, and an honest In-app updates panel. Visit tasks use user-booked dates, distinguish past bookings from recorded completion and recheck changed source instructions. Recent medication activity is labeled as manual events with its 100-record limit, not scheduled doses or adherence. Read-only frontend derivation has behavior tests; existing backend permissions and plan-integrity guards remain authoritative. No timed alerts, clinical verification or new prescription is introduced.

## Engineering

React/TypeScript frontend, FastAPI backend, local SQLite, PyMuPDF, Tesseract when installed, Ollama phi4-mini:latest. Keep documents on this machine. Explicitly distinguish extracted, reviewed and clinically clarified instructions. No claims of clinical validation. Use fictional documents until authentication and deployment security are implemented.

Prefer simple idiomatic code, descriptive names, few dependencies, and comments only for non-obvious decisions. Avoid file-header descriptions and unnecessary abstractions. Explain major design decisions in documentation.
